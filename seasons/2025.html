The Season Story

The champion

Nick Cardillo captured the Belt in 2025 with Wet Reckless, going 9-5 in the regular season as the #1 seed and pinning Parker Williams (TC Tuggers) in the title match. Brian Mulvihill entered the playoffs as the reigning Belt holder.

The title run

The title run: week 16: 174.55-151 over Jacob Maddox; week 17: 140.6-132.95 over Parker Williams.

Title-run trade (week 7): Nick Cardillo acquired Tetairoa McMillan, Travis Etienne Jr. from Ryan Nussdorfer for Jordan Addison, Nico Collins.

Stars & the wire

Carrying the load: Christian McCaffrey (RB) was the season's MVP for Nick Cardillo. 408.9 fantasy points scored in his team's starting lineup for Wet Reckless.

Wire hero: Nicholas Kasik found Dak Prescott (QB). Added in week 3 (waiver), then put up 253.85 starting points for Torts Illustrated.

Draft room

Draft room verdict: Javonte Williams (RB) was the steal of the draft for Nicholas Kasik (Pick 13.05 (#125 overall) → finished RB11 with 244.8 points). On the other end, Brian Mulvihill spent a top pick on Malik Nabers (WR), who returned only 58.6 points (WR97).

Luck

Schedule victim of the year: Nicholas Kasik. Went 3-11 but an all-play record of 53-73 says 5.89 wins were deserved. 1,967.75 points against.

And the horseshoe award goes to Drew Hartman: Went 8-6 on an all-play pace of 6 wins (+2). Only 1,657.65 points against.

The basement

Bringing up the rear: Brian Mulvihill (Wubba Lubba Dub Dubs) finished last in the final standings at 3-11.

Trades

Trade log: 4 trades recorded in the weekly archive (see the transactions section below).

i How to read this
  • All-play record: your score that week against every other team, not just your opponent. In a 10-team league that's 9 games a week. It's the fairest measure of how good your team really was.
  • Expected wins: your all-play win % × the games you actually played. It's what a perfectly fair schedule would have given you.
  • Actual vs expected: the wins you got against the schedule you drew.
  • Luck (+/-): actual minus expected. +2 means the schedule handed you about two extra wins. -2 means it stole about two. Near 0 means your record was fair.

Real example: in 2024, Ryan Nussdorfer went 11-3, but his all-play record was 75-51 (0.595). That's worth 8.33 wins over 14 games, so he was +2.67 lucky. The flip side: Andrew Saur in 2024 went 3-11 with a 0.484 all-play, -3.78.
