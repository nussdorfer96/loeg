The Season Story

The champion

Brian Mulvihill captured the Belt in 2022 with Wubba Lubba Dub Dubs, going 9-5 in the regular season as the #2 seed and pinning Matthew Vinson (DAKstreet Boys) in the title match. Bradley Kochheiser entered the playoffs as the reigning Belt holder.

The title run

The title run: week 16: 117.5-84.46 over Ryan Nussdorfer; week 17: 108.4-82.5 over Matthew Vinson.

Regular season

The regular season belonged to Drew Hartman (What Parents?), the #1 seed at 11-3, but the run ended in week 16 with a 91.8-140.48 loss to Matthew Vinson.

Ryan Nussdorfer led the league in points (1,806.92) and finished #3.

Stars & the wire

Carrying the load: Patrick Mahomes (QB) was the season's MVP for Taylor Alvarado. 402.42 fantasy points scored in his team's starting lineup for East Lansing Nature Boy.

Wire hero: Drew Hartman found Geno Smith (QB). Added in week 9 (waiver), then put up 147.5 starting points for What Parents?.

Draft room

Draft room verdict: Tony Pollard (RB) was the steal of the draft for Drew Hartman (Pick 9.04 (#84 overall) → finished RB7 with 246.9 points). On the other end, Bradley Kochheiser spent a top pick on Javonte Williams (RB), who returned only 42 points (RB78).

Luck

Schedule victim of the year: Andrew Saur. Went 3-11 but an all-play record of 48-78 says 5.33 wins were deserved. 1,680.66 points against.

And the horseshoe award goes to Drew Hartman: Went 11-3 on an all-play pace of 8.67 wins (+2.33). Only 1,534.08 points against.

The basement

Bringing up the rear: Parker Williams (Mo Mooney Mo Problems) finished last in the final standings at 6-8.

Trades

Trade log: 1 trade recorded in the weekly archive (see the transactions section below).

📌 Featured lore

The Championship That Was Never Finished

The 2022 title game (NFL week 17, played January 2023) was Brian Mulvihill vs. Matthew Vinson, and it ran straight into the Bills-Bengals Monday night game on January 2, 2023, the night Damar Hamlin collapsed on the field. The NFL never resumed that game, so the league decided the title itself: the affected players were scored using their averages, with the outlying highs and lows removed to cut down the variance. Brian won and took the Belt.

What ESPN recorded: a 108.4-82.5 final, with every player from the cancelled game credited 0 points. In the starting lineups that meant Joe Burrow for Brian, and Josh Allen, Ja'Marr Chase and Evan McPherson for Matthew. ESPN doesn't store the league's average-based adjusted scores. If anyone kept the adjusted numbers, add them here.

i How to read this
  • All-play record: your score that week against every other team, not just your opponent. In a 10-team league that's 9 games a week. It's the fairest measure of how good your team really was.
  • Expected wins: your all-play win % × the games you actually played. It's what a perfectly fair schedule would have given you.
  • Actual vs expected: the wins you got against the schedule you drew.
  • Luck (+/-): actual minus expected. +2 means the schedule handed you about two extra wins. -2 means it stole about two. Near 0 means your record was fair.

Real example: in 2024, Ryan Nussdorfer went 11-3, but his all-play record was 75-51 (0.595). That's worth 8.33 wins over 14 games, so he was +2.67 lucky. The flip side: Andrew Saur in 2024 went 3-11 with a 0.484 all-play, -3.78.
